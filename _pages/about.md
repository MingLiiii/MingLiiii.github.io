I am a **Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at the University of Maryland**, advised by [Prof. Tianyi Zhou](https://tianyizhou.github.io/). I study how post-training shapes LLM reasoning and behavior, from selecting high-value training data to evaluating the reliability of models and agentic systems.

I am a **[2026 Apple Scholar in AI/ML](https://machinelearning.apple.com/updates/apple-scholars-aiml-2026)**, with industry research experience at Amazon, Microsoft Research, and Adobe, and a visiting research appointment at MBZUAI. I also serve as an *ACL ARR Area Chair* and reviewer for major conferences.

**I expect to graduate in Spring 2027 and am seeking Research Scientist / Applied Scientist / Research Engineer opportunities in industry.** [Download my CV]({{ '/assets/pdf/Ming_CV.pdf' | relative_url }}) or [get in touch](mailto:minglii@umd.edu).

### Research

#### Data-Centric LLM Post-Training

I develop methods to select and reuse high-value training signals, spanning instruction-data selection, data-quality analysis, and selective agent tuning. Representative projects include [IFD / Cherry LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12032) and [Data Quality through Layer-wise Gradients](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10766), connecting data-efficient adaptation with an understanding of how instruction and reasoning data shape post-training.

Related work: [Superfiltering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00530) · [Reflection-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10110) · [ATLaS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02197).

#### Reasoning & Model Behavior

I study how training and inference change reasoning structure, dynamics, and failure modes through [Schoenfeld's Anatomy / ThinkARM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19995) (**ACL 2026 Oral & SAC Highlight**) and [Fast vs. Slow Thinking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23743) (**ACL 2025 Oral**).

Related work: [Missing Premise / Overthinking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06514) · [Visual reasoning RL analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12395).

#### AI Evaluation & Reliability

I design controlled evaluations to uncover capability limits, investigate model and agent behavior, and test the reliability of their judgments. Recent work includes [Moltbook / AI Agent Society](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14299), on social dynamics and collective behavior, and [Rhetoric Reward-Hacking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08975), on how rhetorical framing changes AI reviewers' judgments. My Moltbook research was discussed in a [Science News interview](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/ai-agent-teams-fail-succeed-bots-chaos).

Related work: [ColorBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10514) · [BenTo](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ki7b0qD11r) · [ClawEnvKit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18543).

I also explore **Human-AI Cognitive Alignment**, especially how model reasoning and difficulty judgments relate to human cognition: [Difficulty Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18880), [Item Difficulty Modeling](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/00131644251344973), and [Episode2Difficulty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28186).

I am always happy to discuss research ideas and collaborations.
